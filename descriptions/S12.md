# S12 - Chapter 12: Can we see what gradient descent is doing?

Summary: a tanh neuron with two weights has a loss that can be drawn as a 3D surface, and gradient descent is a ball rolling downhill on it (an analogy for real networks).

## What happens
- Example A: the loss of one tanh neuron (bias 0.5, first two inputs of the S11 dataset) is computed by the engine for every (w1, w2) on a grid and drawn as a surface with contour lines on the floor.
- Example A: at the start (-2.5, 2) the gradient arrow points uphill, the step goes the opposite way, and the ball rolls 25 steps at learning rate 0.05 with the loss counting down from 4.4251 to 3.888.
- Example B: three start points, each tried with learning rate 0.05 and 1.0, with a table of final losses; the huge step overshoots and ends at 7.9576.
- Expert corner: gradient arrows cross every contour at about 90 degrees (computed angle on screen).
- Expert corner: in a saturated corner (-2.5, -2.5) the slope is about 0.003 and the ball barely moves.
- Expert corner: a real network has one slope per weight; S11's 41 gradients are drawn as bars.

## Formulas
- L(w1, w2) = sum over i of (tanh(w1 x1i + w2 x2i + b) - yi)^2
- w1 <- w1 - eta * dL/dw1, with real numbers substituted
- cos(theta) = |grad L . t| / (|grad L| |t|)

## Key takeaways
1. The gradient points uphill and is perpendicular to the contour lines; we step the other way.
2. Small steps converge; a huge step overshoots or flies off the map.
3. Flat (saturated) regions have almost zero gradient, so training stalls there.

## Glossary
- Loss surface: the loss drawn as height over the weights (an analogy).
- Contour line: points with equal loss.
- Plateau: a flat region with almost zero gradient.
- Learning rate: the step size multiplier.
- Gradient: the vector of slopes, one per weight.

## Common mistakes
- Treating the 3D bowl as the real picture: real networks have millions of weights and cannot be drawn.
- Using a big learning rate to go faster, which overshoots and can end worse than the start.
