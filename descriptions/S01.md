# S01 - Chapter 1: What is a derivative, really?

Summary: a derivative is the slope of a curve at one point, found by nudging x by a tiny h and dividing the change in f by h.

## Act A - the lecture's own function
- The notebook's function f(x) = 3x^2 - 4x + 5 is plotted for x from -5 in steps of 0.25 (40 points); f(3) = 20.
- Predict-then-reveal: is f(3+h) above or below 20? A secant with a white step h and an orange rise answers it.
- The camera zooms into the rise-over-run triangle, then h shrinks to 0.001 and the secant turns into the tangent.
- The formula (f(x+h) - f(x)) / h is worked with real numbers: (20.014 - 20) / 0.001 = 14.003.
- A table fills row by row: x = 3 gives 14.003, x = -3 gives -21.997 (sign predicted first), x = 2/3 gives about 0.003 (flat).
- The lecture's warning: with h = 1e-16 the float f(3+h) equals f(3), so the computed slope is 0, not 14.

## Act B - what if?
- The curve morphs into g(x) = x^3 - 3x, whose tangent is flat at x = -1 (hump) and x = 1 (valley): g'(x) = 3x^2 - 3 = 0.
- The curve morphs into |x|: the left secant at 0 gives -1, the right gives +1, so no derivative exists there.
- Back to f with h = 1: the slope reads 17 instead of 14, the secant visibly misses the tangent.

## Act C - expert corner
- Error against the true slope 14 for h = 10^-k on log axes: forward difference vs central difference (f(x+h) - f(x-h)) / (2h).
- At h = 1e-4 the forward error is about 3.0e-4 and the central error about 3.8e-11.
- Below h of about 1e-8 both get worse because floating-point rounding; at h = 1e-16 both slopes are exactly 0.
- Conclusion: micrograd never nudges; every operation stores its exact local derivative.

## Formulas
- slope = (f(x+h) - f(x)) / h
- central difference = (f(x+h) - f(x-h)) / (2h)
- g'(x) = 3x^2 - 3

## Three key takeaways
- The derivative is rise over run for a vanishing step, and its sign says whether f goes up or down.
- A flat tangent (slope 0) marks a hump, a valley or a plateau; a kink has no derivative.
- h must be small but not too small: too big is inaccurate, too tiny is destroyed by rounding.

## Glossary
- Slope: rise over run, how steeply a curve climbs.
- Secant: a line through two points of the curve.
- Tangent: the line that just touches the curve at one point.
- Derivative: the slope of the tangent at one point.
- Floating point: the finite-precision way computers store decimal numbers.

## Two common mistakes
- Using a huge h (such as 1) and trusting the answer: the secant is not the tangent.
- Using a tiny h (such as 1e-16) and trusting the answer: the rounding wipes out the difference and returns 0.
