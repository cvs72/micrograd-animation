## Formulas used
- gradient: the vector of dL/dw for every weight w
- update: w <- w - eta * dL/dw, with the real knob value, learning rate 0.05 and gradient from the engine

## Key takeaways
- A derivative is a slope, a gradient is all the slopes together, and backprop computes them by the chain rule.
- A tiny network of 41 numbers and a giant language model are trained by the same idea.
- Gradient descent turns every knob a little against its gradient.

## Glossary
- Gradient: the vector of slopes of the loss with respect to every parameter.
- Parameter: one knob (weight or bias) that training may change.
- Learning rate: the size of one step along the negative gradient.
- Language model: a network trained to predict the next word or letter.

## Common mistakes
- Thinking that big networks use a different idea: they use the same gradients, only at larger scale.
- Treating the loss surface picture as literal: it is an analogy for two weights only.
