## Audience and promise
A developer with no calculus background. After the video they can explain, to someone else and with their own numbers: what a derivative and a gradient are, what a computation graph is, how the backward pass applies the chain rule node by node, why gradients are added up, how a neuron and a tiny network are built from Value objects, and how gradient descent trains them. They can also say when it goes wrong (saturation, dead ReLU, overflow, learning rate too large, forgetting zero_grad) and how experts think about it. Voice: a patient, enthusiastic teacher; plain words; every new term defined the first time it appears. Target length 30 to 55 minutes in total.

## Source material (MUST be used)
reference/ holds three kinds of source: lecture_notes.md (structured notes on the lecture with the values he states and keywords to grep), lecture_transcript.md (the spoken lecture in numbered chunks [T001], [T002] and so on, cleaned of speech-recognition errors) and the two lecture notebooks as text. Order of use for every scene: (1) read the matching section of reference/lecture_notes.md; (2) grep reference/lecture_transcript.md with the keywords given there to see HOW he explains the idea, in what order, with which analogy; (3) take the exact code, values and order of steps from the notebooks. The NOTEBOOKS WIN for code and numbers because the transcript is live speech with slips, and some values are never said aloud. Example A of every chapter MUST come from these sources. Write the file names and section or chunk numbers you used into progress.md. Reproduce the notebook's example with the SAME variable names (a, b, c, e, d, f, L, x1, x2, w1, w2, b, n, o and so on), the SAME values and the SAME order of steps, so a viewer can follow along with the notebook. The numbers in this plan were checked by running the engine; if a source disagrees with a number in this plan, the SOURCE WINS and you note the difference in progress.md. Never copy the transcript, the notes or the notebook text verbatim into captions or on-screen text; explain in your own words (code panels may show short excerpts in the notebook's own variable names).

## The three-act pattern (MUST be used in S01 to S12)
Every chapter has exactly three acts, in this order, each opened by an act banner held for at least 2.5 seconds and by a first caption that BEGINS with the exact words shown:
ACT A, caption begins "Example A:". The lecture's own example, with its original values. Explain step by step what happens, why, and what the number means.
ACT B, caption begins "Example B:". "What if?": change the values or the setup as the row says. The change MUST be animated live: the numbers morph from the old values to the new ones and the diagram recomputes in front of the viewer, with a before/after comparison. Explain what changed and WHY the result is different.
ACT C, caption begins "Expert corner:". An insight that an expert in micrograd, calculus or neural networks would add, as listed in the row, shown with its own visual and real numbers.
verify.sh fails any scene in ACT_SCENES whose captions/<ID>.srt does not contain all three phrases.

## Global requirements (apply to EVERY scene; the reviewer checks them one by one)
G1 Subtitles, at the highest standard. Captions are on screen at ALL times except on the title card and the recap line. Bottom-centre, white Text, font_size 30, on a semi-transparent black rectangle, at most 2 lines of about 14 words, each on screen at least 2.5 seconds and swapped with FadeOut/FadeIn of about 0.3 s. Captions NEVER cover the diagram. Every caption says what is visible AND why it matters, in plain spoken language, as a teacher would say it aloud; generic captions such as "now we compute" are forbidden. The Narrator helper writes every caption with start and end time to captions/<ID>.srt (overwritten on every render). verify.sh fails a scene with fewer cues than CAPTIONS_REQUIRED.
G2 Written descriptions. Every scene MUST also write descriptions/<ID>.md with at least DESCRIPTIONS_REQUIRED lines: a one-sentence summary; what happens in each act; the formulas used; three key takeaways; a glossary of the terms introduced; two common mistakes. verify.sh checks the line count.
G3 Text on screen, with a hierarchy: scene title 48, act banner 36, body text at least 28, formulas at least 40, captions 30. Every definition appears as a boxed callout held at least 3 seconds. Key words are colour-highlighted inside sentences. No wall of text: at most 2 lines of body text on screen at once besides the caption.
G4 Formulas. ALL maths is typeset with MathTex, never plain Text. Derive formulas STEP BY STEP with TransformMatchingTex or ReplacementTransform, never by cutting to the final line. Colour-code consistently: data BLUE, gradients ORANGE, local derivatives TEAL, constants WHITE. ALWAYS substitute the real numbers on screen before showing a result, for example 2 * (-3) = -6, and keep the working visible for at least 2 seconds. A bare result without working is a failure.
G5 Never text only. Every act contains at least two real visuals chosen from: Axes plot, node-and-arrow computation graph, 3D surface, code panel (Code mobject) with highlighted lines, bar chart, number line, table, network diagram. Formulas and numbers support a visual and never replace it.
G6 Animation craft. Build every idea with Create, Transform, ReplacementTransform, Indicate, Circumscribe, ValueTracker with always_redraw, MoveAlongPath, FadeTransform, TransformMatchingShapes or TransformMatchingTex. Use a camera zoom or pan (MovingCameraScene or frame animation) at least once per scene to look closely at a detail. At most two new elements per beat; hold each reveal at least 1 second. Show causes: when A changes B, animate the change travelling from A to B (gradient particles, pulses, ghost copies). Transitions between acts use FadeTransform or a wipe, never a hard cut.
G7 3D. S02 (Act C), S12 (all acts) and any other scene that benefits MUST use a ThreeDScene with labelled ThreeDAxes, a slowly orbiting camera (begin_ambient_camera_rotation) and add_fixed_in_frame_mobjects so captions and banners stay flat on screen. Assert in code the key results listed in each row, so a wrong number fails the render. Never hardcode a result.
G10 Teaching rules. Each scene opens with a 3 second title card (chapter number and one-line question) and ends with a 4 second recap line. One idea per beat. The first time a term appears (scalar, Value, child, computation graph, derivative, gradient, local derivative, chain rule, topological order, tanh, ReLU, loss, learning rate) show a definition callout. G11 Legibility. Safe margin 0.5 units. Minimum font size 24. Nothing overlaps, nothing is clipped or off screen, plot curves stay inside their axes, axes are labelled with units. Contrast must be readable at 480p. No flashing.
G12 Forbidden, and the reviewer FAILS the scene for any of these: text-only frames; missing or generic captions; formulas shown as plain Text; numbers without working; hardcoded numbers; a missing act or an act banner that is not visible; Act B that does not animate the change; the same layout reused for all three acts with only the values changed; clipped or tiny text; unexplained jargon; claims not backed by numbers on screen; a scene with no predict-then-reveal beat.
